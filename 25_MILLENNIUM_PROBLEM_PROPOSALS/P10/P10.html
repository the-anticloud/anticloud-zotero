### 1. Core Hypothesis
The "Hard Problem"—the difficulty of explaining why physical processes give rise to subjective experience—is resolved not by positing dualism, but by recognizing that **consciousness is a specific topological invariant of high-dimensional information processing**.

Specifically, consciousness arises when a neural system's functional connectivity forms a **non-trivial homotopy group** within its state space, creating a unified causal structure that cannot be decomposed into independent subsystems without loss of informational integrity. This unified structure, termed the **"Qualia Manifold,"** is mathematically distinct from mere data processing. Subjective experience ($\Phi$) is the measure of this manifold's resistance to reduction; it is the geometric shadow cast by the system's irreducible causal power.

* **Key Distinction:** Unlike standard computation (which can be arbitrarily modular), conscious states require a specific *topological knotting* of cause-and-effect relations that prevents the system from being treated as a collection of parts. The "what it feels like" is the intrinsic property of this specific topology.

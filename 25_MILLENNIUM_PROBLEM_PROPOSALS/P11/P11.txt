#### 1. Core Hypothesis
The mechanism driving high-temperature superconductivity ($T_c$) in cuprate perovskites and hydrogen-rich hydrides is not solely electronic (as in the pure spin-fluctuation models) nor purely phononic (as in BCS), but arises from a **resonant coupling between doped charge carriers and specific soft lattice modes near a Quantum Critical Point (QCP)**.

Specifically, we hypothesize that doping drives the system to a QCP where a structural instability (e.g., $c$-axis breathing mode in cuprates or H-H stretching in hydrides) diverges. This divergence creates a "glue" with an energy scale $\Omega^*$ comparable to the Fermi energy $E_F$. Unlike conventional phonons where retardation suppresses pairing, here the spectral weight of these modes shifts into the low-frequency regime while maintaining strong electron-phonon matrix elements due to the non-adiabatic nature of the QCP. This results in a **non-Fermi liquid normal state** that supports robust $d_{x^2-y^2}$ (cuprates) or $s$-wave (hydrides) pairing via a resonant scattering channel, bypassing the McMillan limit.

#### 2. Mathematical Framework

We propose a generalized Eliashberg theory extended to include non-adiabatic quantum critical fluctuations. The central object is the retarded self-energy $\Sigma(k, i\omega_n)$ and the vertex function $\Gamma$.

**A. The Effective Hamiltonian**
$$H = H_{el} + H_{ph} + H_{int}$$
Where $H_{el}$ describes the doped Mott insulator (Hubbard-like) and $H_{ph}$ describes the critical lattice mode. The interaction term $H_{int}$ is modified by a critical exponent $\nu$:
$$H_{int} = \sum_{k,q,\sigma} g(q, T) c^\dagger_{k+q, \sigma} c_{k, \sigma} (b_q + b^\dagger_{-q})$$
Here, $g(q, T) \sim |q|^{-\eta} (T/T_Q)^{-\delta}$ reflects the enhancement near the QCP ($T_Q \to 0$).

**B. Modified Eliashberg Equations**
In the standard Migdal-Eliashberg theory, the mass renormalization $m^*/m \approx 1+\lambda$ assumes adiabaticity ($\omega_D/E_F \ll 1$). In our QCRPM framework, we solve the coupled integral equations for the gap function $\Delta(i\omega_n)$ and the wavefunction renormalization $Z(i\omega_n)$ without the adiabatic approximation:

$$
Z(i\omega_n) = 1 - \frac{1}{\beta Z_N} \sum_m \int dq \frac{\text{Im}\chi(q, \omega_n - \omega_m)}{(\omega_n - \omega_m)^2 + q^2 v_s^2} \left( \frac{|g(q)|^2}{|\omega_n - \omega_m|^2 + \dots} \right)
$$

$$
\Delta(i\omega_n) = \frac{1}{\beta Z_N} \sum_m \int dq \frac{\lambda(q, |\omega_n-\omega_m|)}{|\omega_m|^2 + (\pi T)^2} \frac{\Delta(i\omega_m)}{\sqrt{[\omega_m]^2 + [\Delta(i\omega_m)]^2}}
$$

*Key Deviation:* The susceptibility $\chi(q, \omega)$ follows a dynamical scaling form characteristic of a QCP:
$$ \chi(q, \omega) = \frac{C}{q^{z\nu} + (-i\omega/\Gamma_q)^{\alpha}} $$
where $z=2$ (diffusive) or $z=1$ (Lorentzian) depending on the material class, and $\Gamma_q \propto q^z$.

#### 1. Core Hypothesis
The Cosmological Constant Problem arises from the fundamental misapplication of **equilibrium statistical mechanics** to the vacuum state of quantum fields within an expanding spacetime background.

Current standard theory calculates $\Lambda_{vac}$ by summing zero-point energies ($\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$) up to a cutoff scale (Planck), assuming the vacuum is a static, thermalized ground state. This yields $O(10^{122})$ discrepancy with observation.

**Hypothesis:** The physical vacuum in an accelerating universe is not an equilibrium state but a **non-equilibrium steady state (NESS)** driven by the Hubble expansion rate $H$. In this regime, vacuum energy density does not accumulate linearly with the cutoff scale $\Lambda_{UV}^4$. Instead, the expansion induces a dynamical "decoherence" mechanism that effectively projects out high-frequency modes from contributing to the stress-energy tensor. The observed cosmological constant $\Lambda_{obs}$ is not a bare parameter but an emergent residual term resulting from the balance between vacuum production and Hubble-induced dissipation.

Mathematically, we propose that the effective vacuum energy scales as:
$$ \rho_{eff} \approx \alpha \cdot M_{Pl}^2 H(t)^2 $$
where $\alpha$ is a dimensionless coupling derived from the non-equilibrium entropy production rate, rather than $\rho_{eff} \sim \Lambda_{UV}^4$.

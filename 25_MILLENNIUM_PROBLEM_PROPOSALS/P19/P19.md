**Proposal Title:** The Stochastic Autocatalytic Loop (SAL): A Kinetic Bridge from Prebiotic Chemistry to Darwinian Evolution

### 1. Core Hypothesis
The origin of self-replication did not begin with a single, complex "master molecule" (e.g., the RNA World's specific ribozyme) nor purely random assembly. Instead, it emerged through **Stochastic Autocatalytic Loops (SALs)** within spatially constrained micro-environments (e.g., hydrothermal pore networks or evaporating tidal pools).

In this framework, life is defined as a transition in a chemical system where the **information-to-mass ratio** becomes positive due to the emergence of a closed catalytic cycle that outcompetes background thermal noise. Specifically, we posit that short oligomers ($N < 20$ monomers) formed stochastic catalytic interactions where Product $A$ accelerates its own formation from precursors $P_1...P_k$, creating a non-linear kinetic feedback loop. When these loops become coupled (hypercycles) and encapsulated by amphiphilous boundaries, they cross the threshold for selection.

**Key Distinction:** Unlike traditional "RNA-first" models requiring high-fidelity replication immediately, SAL allows for **low-fidelity, error-prone autocatalysis** initially. Selection acts on the *kinetic stability* of the network topology rather than the sequence fidelity of individual strands until a critical complexity threshold is reached.

### 2. Mathematical Framework

We model the prebiotic soup as a set of interacting species $x_i(t)$ representing concentrations of molecular templates. The dynamics are governed by a generalized replicator equation augmented with synthesis rates, degradation, and stochastic fluctuations.

#### 2.1 The Kinetic Master Equation
For a population of $M$ distinct molecular types in a volume $V$:
$$ \frac{dx_i}{dt} = k_{syn,i}(x) - k_{deg,i} x_i + \eta_i(t) $$

Where:
*   $k_{syn,i}(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \alpha_{ij} x_j^{\beta} f(x_j, \text{template}_i)$ represents autocatalytic production. Here, $\alpha_{ij}$ is the catalytic efficiency matrix, and $\beta$ is the reaction order (typically $\beta > 1$ for autocatalysis, implying super-linear growth).
*   $k_{deg,i}$ accounts for hydrolysis, UV damage, and dilution.
*   $\eta_i(t)$ represents intrinsic stochastic noise (fluctuations in collision frequency), modeled as Gaussian white noise scaled by $\sqrt{x_i}$.

#### 2.2 The Eigen-Quasispecies Transition
To analyze the emergence of dominant sequences, we utilize the Jacobian matrix $J$ of the system at equilibrium. The condition for a stable autocatalytic state (the "Eigenvalue Threshold") requires:
$$ \lambda_{max}(J) > \delta_{avg} $$
Where $\lambda_{max}$ is the largest eigenvalue of the interaction matrix $\mathbf{A}$ (where $A_{ij} \propto \alpha_{ij}$) and $\delta_{avg}$ is the average decay/dilution rate.

If $\lambda_{max} \leq \delta_{avg}$, the system dissolves into a "death valley" of random polymers. If $\lambda_{max} > \delta_{avg}$, an exponential amplification occurs, selecting for the eigenvector corresponding to $\lambda_{max}$. This eigenvector represents the "winning" chemical composition.

#### 2.3 Error Catastrophe Boundary
Let $\mu$ be the per-base mutation/error rate during template copying. For a genome of length $L$, the information content is preserved only if:
$$ \mu L < \ln(2) $$
However, in the early SAL phase, we hypothesize a mechanism where **combinatorial coupling** reduces the effective $L$ required for viability. We define an "effective information capacity" $I_{eff}$:
$$ I_{eff} = L \cdot (1 - H(\mu)) $$
Where $H(\mu)$ is the binary entropy function. The system transitions to evolution when $I_{eff}$ exceeds the minimum bits required to encode a metabolic closure.
